# Bid Narrative

The persuasive opening of any Louie bid response. Procurement register —
declarative, analytical, no marketing soundbites. Use this as the
executive summary / statement of need at the front of a bid; the
technical answer follows in [`product-spec.md`](./product-spec.md).

The marketing-register version of these moves lives in
[`POSITIONING.md`](./POSITIONING.md) under "PEACE scripts." Same logic,
different voice, different audience.

---

## Five-beat executive opening

The structure follows PEACE (Problem / Empathy / Answer / Change / End
Result), rendered in procurement voice. Anchor phrase across the whole
bid: **"act with full context"**.

> **The need.** Mississauga's video records of committee meetings are
> technically open to the public, but they remain illegible in practice —
> for residents trying to follow how their city decides things, and for
> councillors trying to act with full context. Hours of recording, no
> effective search, no way to trace a topic across years or committees.
>
> **The constraint.** The City already publishes every meeting and
> supporting document. The work of opening the record has been done.
> What's missing is a layer that makes the record usable at the speed
> and depth modern civic engagement requires.
>
> **What Louie does.** Louie indexes Mississauga's full council record —
> video, minutes, agendas, staff reports — and exposes it through a
> public search interface. Every answer it returns is cited back to the
> exact transcript line, document section, or video timestamp.
>
> **The change.** Where the current record requires hours of manual
> review to answer a single question, Louie returns a cited answer in
> seconds. The record becomes searchable not just by keyword, but by
> question and topic, across committees and across years.
>
> **The outcome.** Residents can follow what's going on in their city.
> Councillors can act with full context. The City's investment in
> publishing the record begins to compound, instead of sitting in an
> archive that few can navigate.

## Shorter variants for cover letter and slide use

### One-paragraph cover-letter version

> Mississauga's video records of committee meetings are technically open
> to the public, but illegible in practice — both for residents trying
> to follow what their city is doing and for councillors trying to act
> with full context. Louie is a search and explanation layer that
> indexes the City's full council record and returns cited answers in
> seconds. We propose to deliver Louie under the specification and
> pricing detailed in the attached.

### Two-line opener for a deck or pitch slide

> *Mississauga publishes everything. Almost nobody can use it.*
>
> *Louie is the layer that closes that gap.*

### Single-sentence summary for a procurement abstract

> Louie indexes a municipality's full council record — video, minutes,
> agendas, and staff reports — and returns cited answers in seconds, so
> residents and councillors can act on the record the City has already
> made public.

## Anchor language to use throughout the bid

These phrases are load-bearing. They appear in the executive opening and
should be echoed in section headers, sub-headlines, and the conclusion
to keep the bid feeling unified.

| Phrase | Where it earns its keep |
|---|---|
| **"act with full context"** | The anchor. Use whenever describing what councillors gain. |
| **"technically open, illegible in practice"** | Names the gap. Use in the need / current-state section. |
| **"the City's investment in publishing the record"** | Honours work already done. Use when justifying scope. |
| **"cited back to the exact transcript line"** | Names the trust mechanism. Use in capability descriptions. |
| **"the record begins to compound"** | The transformation. Use in outcomes / closing. |

## Register notes

### What works in bid voice

- **Declarative situation statements.** "Mississauga's video records are
  technically open…" rather than "Have you ever wondered why…?"
- **Measured acknowledgment.** "We recognize that…" / "We understand the
  constraints of…" — never "and we get it."
- **Specific, bounded scope language.** "Louie indexes the full council
  record" — not "Louie helps your city become…"
- **Verifiable claims.** "Cited back to the exact transcript line" — not
  "answers you can trust." Trust is earned by the citation, not asserted.
- **Honouring work already done.** Bids land when they treat the city as
  competent and the gap as structural, not as a failure of the
  procurement officer's predecessors.

### What to avoid in bid voice

- **Rhetorical questions.** *"Is it hard to…?"* / *"Have you ever…?"* —
  fine in cold email, wrong here. The procurement officer is not your
  audience for hooks; they're your audience for analysis.
- **Marketing imperatives.** *"Put Louie on your council record"* /
  *"Point Louie at…"* — out. Replace with declarative scope.
- **Effusive empathy.** *"…and we get it"* — out. Replace with
  acknowledgment.
- **Status play.** *"Be the city that…"* — reads as flattery and risks
  insulting the buyer. Skip.
- **Triplet cadences in the opening.** *"Search, follow, and trust"* —
  earns its keep in marketing copy, weakens the analytical register at
  the top of a bid. Save for the body, where examples and capability
  descriptions can take it.
- **Em dashes used for emphasis.** Use them for parenthetical clarity
  only. Bids that lean on em-dash drama read as decks pretending to be
  documents.

## How this fits with the rest of the bid

1. **Cover letter** — one paragraph, version above.
2. **Executive summary** — the five-beat opening, as written.
3. **Statement of need** — expanded version of "The need" beat, with
   evidence (eScribe search returning meetings rather than answers, the
   hours required to trace a single decision, the engagement gap).
4. **Proposed solution** — pulls from `product-spec.md` §1–4.
5. **Capability and architecture** — pulls from `product-spec.md` §3.
6. **Pricing** — pulls from `PRICING.md` verbatim.
7. **Timeline** — pulls from `product-spec.md` §8.
8. **Closing** — restates "the outcome" beat in the buyer's voice
   (*"Residents will follow… councillors will act with full context…
   the City's investment will compound"*).

If the bid is opened to a competitive RFP rather than awarded as an
unsolicited bid, the executive opening becomes the differentiator. The
spec answers *can we deliver*; the narrative answers *why this*.
